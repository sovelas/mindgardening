A Christian duality. What is *sacred*? What is *profane*? 
*This* world is the realm of the profane, a fallen place in need of saving. The sacred is transcendent and separate; the profane is where we live in.

This dualistic structure runs parallel to other christian binaries: good and evil, being and non-being. They make believe that the divine is fundamentally elsewhere, and the task of the religious person is to bridge the gap. Through belief, through moral conduct, through grace. The world itself is not the locus of the sacred but an obstacle or testing ground on the path to it.

[[Friedrich Schelling]] argued that this dualism may not be permanent. In his vision of the coming [[schelling-non-dualistic-christianity|Age of John]], the distinction between sacred and profane would dissolve by recognizing that they co-constitute each other, much like the [[duck-rabbit-as-metaphor-for-experiential-framing|duck and rabbit]]. The [[kyoto-school-project-overview|Kyoto School]], drawing on [[Zen Buddhism]] and [[Sunyata]], would go further still, arguing that this dissolution is not a future hope but an immanent reality available right now through practice.
