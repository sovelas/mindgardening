20th century history contains plenty competing [[ideology|ideologies]] living in the wake of the [[god-is-dead-as-regretful-warning|death of God]]. When [[religion]] could no longer serve as the primary source of [[meaning]], [[identity]], and moral certainty, [[ideology]] stepped in to fill the same psychological functions: belonging, purpose, an enemy to oppose, a narrative that makes sense of the world.

[[nationalism|Nationalism]], [[Marxism]], [[capitalism]], [[fascism]], etc, became the new religions/tribal allegiances. 
A vise grip on how things are, 
relief from [[cognitive dissonance]], 
a clear sense of who "we" are and who "they" are. 

[[Nietzsche]] saw this coming; the French existentialists ([[Sartre]], [[Simone de Beauvoir]], [[Albert Camus]]) lived through its consequences in Nazi-occupied France.

This transition tells us a story about how the need for group identity and moral certainty is so deep that removing one outlet guarantees another will take its place. Whether that replacement is more or less destructive than what it replaced is questionable. 

Would it be possible to turn mainstream other [[responses-to-the-void]]?
