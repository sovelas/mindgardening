I can't write the way I used to. It used to be easy — emotion bubbling straight onto the page. Now the calculated, short words are the first ones through, and the fizzy feelings can't keep up. I'm full of a hunger for *synthesis and clarity* that the old outpouring can't satisfy.

I can't tell whether that's growth or just [[adults-narrowing-vocabulary|narrowing]]. Probably both: clarity is a kind of seeing, but it's bought by cutting.

In the off chance I find myself writing poetry, I get caught: why write the cut-down thoughts *like this* — with rhythm and musicality, in cramped little forms — instead of giving myself a bigger room to speak in plain prose? Wouldn't that be easier, less restrictive? Maybe. But the constraint forces the synthesis, makes me find the one shape that holds the feeling instead of spilling or blabing around it. Maybe musicality is a way I [[coincidence-as-beauty|compose the thought down to something true]]. Or that's the bet. This whole garden is a bet that I can make something true emerge out of a page.
