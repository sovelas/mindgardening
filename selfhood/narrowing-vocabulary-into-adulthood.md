Becoming an adult is, effectively, narrowing your vocabulary after an exhausting effort to broaden it, until you can no longer parse the openness of a child's speech.

My nephews ask "could we take the train to Paris?" and the adult reflex is to answer no, even though we clearly can if some conditions are met (but that I don't want to have to explain). [[nuance-versus-the-straight-answer|At work I'm coached the same way]] — strip the nuance, give the clean answer people can follow easy. 

I don't want to narrow. I feel something slipping when I'm flattening the world. You stop being able to *hear* possibilities that don't survive translation into your existing concepts. But I don't want to disintegrate either, to keep every branch and never manage to say anything. I'm searching for vocabulary wide enough to keep hearing but disciplined enough to still mean something.
