The [[The Aesthetic Attitude|aesthetic attitude]] resembles [[scientific inquiry]]. Both seem to ask a person to suspend bias and approach things "as they are." Both value distance from immediate impulse and distrust purely subjective reaction.

Science, however, tries to manage bias through method, replication, criticism, and shared standards. In that sense, it assumes situated knowers and builds procedures to correct for them.

Beauvoir's critique is about this fantasy about a "view from nowhere." The problem is not disciplined inquiry, but the illusion that one can become a neutral spectator untouched by history or standpoint.

This makes the aesthetic attitude or scientific inquiry, when it hardens into false [[objectivity]], an existential danger. Detachment can become a way of avoiding involvement, commitment, and responsibility.
