The aesthetic attitude is the attempt to encounter a work of art in a detached and disinterested way. In the history of [[aesthetics]] and [[philosophy of art]], this often means trying to suspend personal preferences, practical concerns, and prior assumptions in order to see the work "as it is."  
  
This ideal treats aesthetic judgment as something that becomes purer the more one removes bias and subjective involvement. It assumes that beauty can be perceived most clearly by a spectator who approaches the work as a kind of blank slate.  
  
[[Simone de Beauvoir]] treats this ideal with suspicion, especially when it becomes a model for how people relate not just to art but to the world more broadly.
