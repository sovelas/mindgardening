Accepting finitude, risk, uncertainty, and responsibility instead of seeking refuge in a system that promises final reassurance. 

The good life is surely not serene metaphysical peace; it is a life of projects, struggles, and a kind of joy that exists **through** uncertainty rather than beyond it.

An ethics can be built on ambiguity. But [[camus-against-philosophical-escape|it will not look like an ethics grounded in abstract absolutes]], God, or eternal essences. Those let us evade responsibility and flee the anxiety of ambiguity. 

The basis of ethics is freedom as something lived in situations: evil lies in denying freedom, and ethical action aims to sustain both [[freedom-depends-on-others-freedom|my freedom and others’ freedom]]. 

Ethics therefore requires resisting [[oppression-as-enemy-of-freedom|oppression]] and helping create the material and [[|political conditions]] in which others can act as free beings too. So the foundation is neither certainty nor rule-worship, but a shared, fragile, ongoing practice of liberation.

[[The struggle is never finished]], the future stays open, and action always carries risk. But for [[Simone de Beauvoir]], that is [[ambiguity-as-truth-of-ethics|not a defect in ethics; it is the truth of ethics]]. So her final answer is something like: **yes, one can live well and even happily within ambiguity, provided one gives up the dream of purity, accepts responsibility, and wills a freedom that includes others.**
