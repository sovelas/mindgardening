[[Simone de Beauvoir]] argues that [[the-adventurer]] ultimately fails because they deny one side of a fundamental duality: the fact that you are simultaneously an individual with personal projects *and* a member of society at large. The adventurer lives as though their life is self-contained. They create meaning, work on projects, and when they die, those projects die with them. 

BUT!

The adventurer stands on the shoulders of giants: the range of choices available to them exists only because people in the past willed their freedom (see [[freedom-depends-on-others-freedom]]). 

Countless people in the present go to work every day, sustaining the conditions that make the adventurer's projects possible. 

The effects of anyone's projects extend beyond their death. Your [[freedom-outlives-the-individual|freedom lives on]], planting seeds for the next generation. 

The adventurer's self-contained picture is just not real. And it is within this "only willing my own freedom" attitude, Beauvoir warns, that most tyrannies emerge.
