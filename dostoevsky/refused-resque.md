[[myshkin-as-holy-fool|Prince Myshkin]] offers [[nastasia-philipovnas-transformation|Nastasia Philipovna]] everything: marriage, respect, refusal to judge her past, a fortune. 

"You have suffered, you have passed through hell and emerged pure, and that is very much." 

She knows it's genuine.
"No one has ever spoken to me like that before".
She confesses she has dreamed of exactly such a person for five years, and yet she refuses him. 

"You thought I should accept this good child's invitation to ruin him?" She chooses [[rogojin-as-passion-incarnate|Rogojin]] instead: a man she does not love, whose company she knows is dangerous. She does it to punish herself rather than contaminating someone she recognizes as good.
She is trapped in [[internalized-shame|internalized shame]]. Accepting love feels like a betrayal of one's own self-knowledge. 

If you believe yourself damaged, then the person offering rescue must be either deceived (in which case accepting would be exploitation) or deluded (in which case you would destroy them). 

Nastasia's refusal is not exactly modesty. More of a stubborn and tragic logic: she has absorbed the world's verdict on her so completely that she can only act in accordance with it. This related to the clinical concept of [[negative therapeutic reaction]] in [[psychoanalysis]] (patients worsen precisely when treatment succeeds, because improvement contradicts their core belief). 

The prince offers her a story in which she is innocent; while she cannot live in a story she does not believe in.
