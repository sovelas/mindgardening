[[Thomas Nagel|Nagel]] asks *what is it like to be a bat?* — and his point is that no pile of objective fact about echolocation gets you to the bat's *experience*; there is something it is like to be it, reachable only from the inside. I keep extending the question: *what is it like to be Dostoevsky?* (the thought came while reading his *Diary of a Writer*, p.91).

The bat version is about the gap *between* species; the Dostoevsky version is the same gap *within* the species, and somehow more haunting for it. I can read every word he wrote — more of his inner life than I have of almost anyone's — and still not arrive at the *being-him*. If subjectivity stays that sealed even between two humans, one of them soaked in the other's words, then the closeness I can reach with anyone is always a reconstruction, never the thing itself.

It connects to [[the-gap-between-symbols-and-the-real|the gap between symbols and the real]] — words are the most of a person I can have, and they are still only symbols — and it's why [[should-humanity-get-a-special-tag|the question of who gets a moral tag]] stays so slippery: we can't verify the inside of *each other*, let alone a bat or a machine. The privacy of the inside is the wall I keep walking into. See also [[shame-internalized-as-identity]].
