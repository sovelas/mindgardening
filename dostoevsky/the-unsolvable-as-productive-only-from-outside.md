Many thinkers insist that some seemingly unsolvable condition (the [[the-gap-between-symbols-and-the-real|gap]], the [[ambiguity-of-human-existence|ambiguity]], the [[the-absurd-in-camus|absurd]], the [[the-lack-in-human-existence|lack]]) is not merely tolerable but *generative*: [[Slavoj Žižek|Žižek]] says the gap is the space where critical thinking and [[creativity]] emerge, [[Simone de Beauvoir|Beauvoir]] says the lack is the condition of [[freedom-as-foundation-of-ethics|freedom]], [[Dostoevsky]] says suffering reveals skills and perspectives otherwise inaccessible. But the [[the-crush-as-philosophical-state|crush note]] remembers me the person *inside* the unsolvable condition almost never experiences it as productive.

The crush is the ordinary person's encounter with the gap between [[symbols]] and [[the Real]] (the beloved is unknowable, the outcome undecidable, the condition structurally unresolvable) and what it produces is not critical thinking or creative self-making but overanxious paralysis, [[crystallization-in-love|crystallization]], irrational obsession, etc. The existing synthesis on [[why-steering-into-the-absurdity-requires-what-both-pathologies-destroy|Cioran's prescription]] sharpens the point: the very consciousness required to inhabit the absurd productively is the consciousness that makes it unbearable. Dostoevsky claims suffering reflected on *in a specific way* opens new perspectives, but the qualifier does all the work and neither he nor [[Albert Camus|Camus]] gives a reliable account of how one arrives at that specific way from inside the condition itself. Camus's [[camus-critique-of-philosophical-systems|critique of systems]] is devastating, but his alternative, a new way of being that doesn't require grounding in abstractions, is described from the philosopher's desk, not from the floor of the experience. The unsolvable may indeed be the space where freedom, creativity, and genuine thought become possible, but that possibility seems to be visible primarily in retrospect and from the outside. 

Easier said than done.
