Different existential thinkers respond differently to the experience of the void.

For [[emil-cioran|Cioran]], the collapse of meaning leaves one with [[nothingness-as-ciorans-central-premise|nothingness]], and the task is not to overcome it but to live on without justification, cultivating a love of absolute uselessness.

[[Albert Camus]] responds with [[revolt-against-the-absurd|revolt]] and [[lucidity-over-meaning|lucidity]]: one must live without illusion, yet refuse resignation.

[[Jean-Paul Sartre]] treats the absence of given meaning as the condition of radical [[freedom]]: human beings must choose, act, and take responsibility for what they become.

[[simone de beauvoir|Simone de Beauvoir]] emphasizes [[ambiguity]] and ethics: freedom is lived in concrete situations with others, so the response to groundlessness must include responsibility toward other freedoms.

[[Fyodor Dostoevsky]] responds to the void not by accepting groundlessness, but by treating it as a spiritual crisis that can only be overcome through faith, suffering, humility, and [[active-love-in-dostoevsky|active love]].

[[slavoj-zizek|Žižek]] treats the void not as a problem to respond to, but as a constitutive lack at the heart of subjectivity and social reality; it was already there to begin with; act from within contradiction.
