From the [[a-cento|cento]]: the demons and shadows of the past are *the same as the war against death* — "always a beautiful, and noble, and wonderful, and glorious thing; always hopeless and quixotic too." My internal wars will never be over or won, and that doesn't make life flat and dull; *we live to fear death and then again to love it.*

I want to keep both halves and refuse the usual collapse. [[ciorans-acceptance|Cioran]] would press the hopeless half until the nobility starts to look like self-deception; a cheap optimism would keep the nobility and quietly drop the hopelessness. But the fragment insists on holding them at once: the war is worth waging *because* it can't be won, not in spite of it — quixotic and glorious turn out to be the same fact seen from two sides.

That's a different answer than the ones I've gathered under [[responses-to-the-void|responses to the void]]: not meaning found, not meaning denied, but meaning *in the waging* — the fighting itself, taken up without any promise of victory, is where the beauty lives. Closest, maybe, to [[cioran-on-suicide|deciding to stay]] for no reason that would satisfy an accountant.
