[[Friedrich Schelling]] places absolute nothingness, what he calls the *[[Unground]]* or the groundless ground, at the center of his philosophy of religion. This nothingness is not just absence but the conditions that allow existence to unfold into the future. There is no independent existence of people, things, or nature... all of these are part of a dynamic, self-revealing process that is constantly moving toward... something.

Similar to the [[Sunyata|emptiness of Sunyata]] in [[Buddhism]], both point to a groundless ground underlying all things. However, the key difference is that for Schelling, the process has a *direction*: a [[teleology]]. All things participate in a progressive unfolding, and this progression toward something is central to his entire system.

One implication is that human history is really the study of how human consciousness has unfolded as one piece of this universal process. We can never understand the *totality* of absolute nothingness through abstract concepts, but human events and ideas are an access point for studying small pieces of it.
