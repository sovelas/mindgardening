The novel's final act is a sequence of purchase orders. Candide buys Cacambo's freedom, then Pangloss's and the Baron's from the galley, then Cunegonde's and the old woman's. Each transaction negotiatiated with a different intermediary: Turkish skipper, Levantine captain, Jewish diamond buyers. Freedom is a commodity with fluctuating market prices.

The old woman's autobiography traces the same mechanism across the Mediterranean: sold six times in a single paragraph, her passage through owners compressed into a single sentence of transactions. The syntax is dehumanizing (a grammatical object passed between verbs). The slave economy requires no central authority; it operates through distributed market logic spanning the Mediterranean, Ottoman Empire, and European continent.

Voltaire constructs a world in which the distinction between slavery and freedom is economic.

Resembles [[commodification]] and [[Marx]]'s critique of bourgeois freedom as merely formal. Free in law, unfree in material reality.
