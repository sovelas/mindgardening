*A passage I assembled and lightly modified from dear lines in six books — The Alchemist, Steppenwolf, Normal People, Man's Search for Meaning, Frankenstein, and The Unbearable Lightness of Being. A cento: the words are mostly theirs, the joins are mine.*

---

One might believe that everything was for the best, even if it meant he lay underground. The abuse I'd got through couldn't bother me any longer by then, but it saddened me all the same — it made me worked up and admonished and incited, and it robbed me of my peace of mind and better feelings. It was the end of it all, I thought; it should cast out all the demons. But there I was, at war with my past as strongly as ever.

In a stroke of luck we met then — the demons were there too. We went on talking for a long time as the sun began to set; we were sharing effortlessly, and I was shocked at myself for being able to do so. There had been a time when I thought I could be plenty happy keeping the demons and their shadows to myself, but I could never have been as happy as I was then, letting you in.

I saw clearly: of course there will be demons and shadows, of course one can be sad about it — it's just the same as the war against death. It's always a beautiful, and noble, and wonderful, and glorious thing; it's always hopeless and quixotic too. My life wouldn't be flat and dull even though my internal wars will never be over or won; we live to fear death and then again to love it —

and so we went along together, in a sense with a lot to do. I looked at you and took your hand in mine and told you to never lie to me, and then we went dancing and laughed like children. I had written my thoughts about you down extensively, but that alone couldn't make me make sense of them. Dancing was as easy as thinking, and it cleaned things up. For once I was an intellectual at home in reality.

Back at your place we just sat still for a long time, soft music urging us to close the distance and achieve total communication. The crystallizing realization that most people go through their whole lives without ever feeling this close to anyone. For the first time in my life I saw the truth as so many poets have set it into song: love is the ultimate and highest goal to which man can aspire. My salvation was through love and in love; and love was as strong as death. In a quiet, solitary moment apart from you, I too felt the urge to set that truth into song, in contemplation of my now beloved. I woke before dawn, the thoughts of you hidden outside the realm of my intelligence and beyond its reach again.

I pressed the keys and let them move me, and I quivered, attentive to the extraordinary thing happening inside me, filling me with a precious essence. The truth was not in the melody but in me — the memories flowing weightless, your smell and taste lingering like kind souls. All of it acquiring the form and solidity of a song; my life also taking shape like notes on a stave, guided by a sense of beauty, transformed by a fortuitous occurrence that turned into a motif and assumed a permanent place in the composition of my life.

Without realizing it, I was composing my life according to the laws of beauty.

I did not trust the fascination I had for these coincidences, but I was attentive to them all the same. It would be wrong to deprive myself of this dimension of beauty, so I let myself be moved —

If our impulses were confined to hunger and thirst only, we might nearly be free. But we are moved by every wind that blows, and by chance words or scenes that those words may convey to us. And if you pretend not to have heard what your heart tells you, it will always be there inside you, repeating what you think about life and about the world. You'll never be able to escape your heart. You taught me that it's better to listen to what it has to say.

You brought me goodness like a gift,
and now it belongs to me.
