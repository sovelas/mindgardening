[[Stendhal on Love|Stendhal]] observes that when we give up on a powerful desire, there is often relief but also emptiness. The will has been raging through the psyche, consuming attention and energy, and when it suddenly stops, it leaves a vacuum behind. 

This is a smaller and more circumscribed instance of a broader pattern: 
when something that organized inner life and gave it direction disappears, disorientation follows. 
The loss of [[religion|religious faith]] or disillusionment with a career path can produce a similar structure, even if at a different scale.

In different ways, [[Nietzsche]]'s [[the-void-after-god|void after God]], [[the-lack-in-human-existence|Beauvoir's permanent lack]], and [[Schopenhauer]]'s [[account of the will]] each describe related forms of emptiness, loss, or instability. 

How deeply human consciousness depends on organizing [[structures of desire and meaning]]...
