The load-bearing line in the [[a-cento-on-love-and-the-war-against-death|cento]]: *love is the ultimate and highest goal to which man can aspire.* It's [[Viktor Frankl|Frankl's]], from *Man's Search for Meaning* — and the staggering thing is *where* he found it. In a camp, with nothing left to possess or do, he came to see that the salvation of man is through love and in love; that a person who has nothing left can still be blessed, if only for a moment, in the contemplation of the beloved. "Love is as strong as death" stops being a poetic flourish and turns nearly literal — the one thing that holds when everything else has been stripped.

What moves me is that this isn't love-as-feeling or love-as-getting. It's love as *attention to a particular person*, which survives even their absence — you can contemplate a beloved who isn't there and still be filled. That's the same root as [[empathy-as-exploit-inverts-attention-as-sacrament|attention as the thing that breaks affliction]], and the exact opposite of [[emptiness-after-desire-passes|the emptiness that follows mere desire]]: desire empties once it's satisfied; this doesn't, because it was never about acquisition in the first place.
