For [[Slavoj Žižek]], drawing on [[Jacques Lacan]], there is a gap between what [[symbols]] can explain about reality and what Lacan called [[the Real]]  (the full, unmediated complexity of existence). This gap exists between our constant search for truth and objective truth itself, between whatever meaning we manage to construct in our lives and any universal meaning that might exist. There will always be a *lack*, a *negativity*, at the core of human experience.

This gap is [[seeing-things-as-puzzles-to-be-solved|not a problem to be solved]] but a structural feature of [[subjectivity]]. 

Ideologies are designed to make this gap disappear by offering a simple and seemingly fully explained version of the world, aka the Truth.

Žižek insists that this **gap is useful**. It is the space within which genuine critical thinking, creativity, and political possibility can emerge. But only if one resists the temptation to close it.
