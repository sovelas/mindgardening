At work I keep hearing that I shouldn't present things with doubt and nuance. Asked "is this possible?", I'm not supposed to say "yes, but only if…" — I should give a straight answer people can follow.

It reminds me of my nephews. They ask "could we take the train to Paris?" and the advice is: don't say *yes, we could, but it would cost so much time and money that it's a bad idea* — just manage the expectation. Oddly, I think I understand the *kids* better than the managers. The child's question is open, and the honest answer is open too. Flattening it to yes/no isn't clarity, it's amputation: you keep the limb that's legible and lose the one that was true.

[[the-gap-between-symbols-and-the-real|Symbols always simplify the real]]; a clean answer is a symbol sanded down until it travels well. Sometimes that's a mercy — people do have to act, and "it depends" can be its own cowardice. But the reflex to strip nuance *by default* is exactly how a mind [[narrowing-vocabulary-into-adulthood|narrows]], and how an organisation slowly loses the ability to think the thoughts its own clean answers leave out.
