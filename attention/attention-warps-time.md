I've lived at the Vellingen hoef about four months, and it already feels like my whole life. I don't think I've once, in thirty years, sat and thought *that* about anywhere.

The difference is attention. The care you give a place, the notice you pay to what's around you, completely warps how time is *felt* — four tended months can hold more than thirty half-watched years. And the unsettling corollary: a life lived on autopilot, paying the minimum attention needed to avoid the minimum discomfort, is not a slightly worse version of the same life. It's a different order of magnitude — an *infinite* difference, it feels like from inside.

[[the-girl-on-the-train|The girl on the train]] gets more out of one window than the adults get out of the whole commute. Same minutes, different lives. This is [[happiness-as-attention|happiness as a skill of perception]] measured the other way round — not in mood, but in how much of your own time you actually inhabit.
