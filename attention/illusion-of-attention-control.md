I notice I don't feel in control of my own attention sometimes — and then I notice something stranger: the rare small moments when it's *allowed to wander* are the ones where I feel *most* in control of it.

For maybe 95% of the day my thoughts run on a tight rail. It's still technically my choice where they go, but it isn't where they'd go if left alone. The freedom isn't in steering hard; it's in the few gaps where the steering stops and attention drifts toward what it actually wants — and those gaps feel more *mine* than all the directed hours combined.

So control and freedom come apart here. The directed attention is controlled but not free; the wandering attention is free but not controlled. [[real-freedom-as-attention-and-discipline|Real freedom might be a discipline of attention]] rather than an absence of constraint — but discipline aimed at *clearing room for* the wandering, not at replacing it. [[freedom-as-imperative|If freedom is the thing to maximise]], the honest question is which of the two kinds I want more of, and whether a life can be arranged to hold more of the wandering without [[from-feeling-to-synthesis|dissolving into noise]].
