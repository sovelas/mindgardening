Rush-hour train, ten adults on a cabin and one little girl. As far as I can tell, only two people are actually *there*: the girl, balancing her feet over and over, watching the objects in the window go by — and me, watching her, delighted.
An I think of [[happiness-as-attention|Myshkin]], insisting on that the richness isn't in the events but in the seeing. The adults all have somewhere to be. Presence is a thing we trade away. The child hasn't learned to trade yet.
