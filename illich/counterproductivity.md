[[Ivan Illich]]'s idea of **counterproductivity**: once a beneficial process crosses a certain threshold of institutionalisation, it begins to undermine the very goal it was built for. Medicine past the threshold *produces* sickness (iatrogenesis); schooling past it produces dependence and a sense of one's own ignorance; fast transport past it eats more time than it saves, once you count the hours worked to pay for the speed. Interesting: the good doesn't merely plateau, it can even invert.

The hinge seems to be to him institutionalisation: the tool stops being something people *use* and becomes something that "uses them", manufacturing needs that only it can meet. Illich calls this a "radical monopoly."

This is the end road of [[just-because-we-can|doing more because we can]]. Capacity slipping past the threshold unnoticed because no single increment looks like the tipping point. It rhymes with [[emotivist-characters|MacIntyre]]: institutions hollowing out the practices they were meant to carry. And it's the strongest *non-romantic* case for the [[government-as-domination|anarchist suspicion of scale]] — not that institutions are evil, but that past a certain size they reliably turn against their own purpose.
