Affliction (*malheur*) is [[simone-weil]]'s term for a state of spiritual malaise, hopelessness, and dehumanization that some people can come to accept as the permanent condition of their existence. Not like ordinary suffering or unhappiness. A deeper condition in which people internalize the belief that they are at best a means to an end, not ends in themselves. They are trapped.

In her [[weil-in-the-factory|firsthand experience in factories]], she witnessed the psychological and spiritual degradation that are born out of labor conditions that prioritize [[quota-over-the-person|production quotas over human dignity]] and render [[thinking-as-antithetical-to-labor|thinking itself impossible]]. This is the affliction.

It appears wherever people are [[people-reduced-to-things|transformed from human beings into things]]/objects, anyone caught in systems of [[force-in-human-events|force]] that reduce persons to resources.

The afflicted are then grouped into collectives that are easier to control, rendered incapable of thinking their way out of the situation, and made to feel grateful for the degraded status they occupy. 

Affliction names not just the suffering itself but the entire psychological traps: the learned helplessness, the abandonment of thought, and the internalized belief that they deserve no better.
