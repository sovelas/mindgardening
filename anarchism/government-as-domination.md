A common defense of [[government]] is that it mediates between all the conflicting interests that make up a society, and so it is what allows people with differing opinions to coexist. Some anarchists would argue that the government does not mediate; it dominates. It is the entity that holds a gun to your head while passing laws that restrict your freedoms, and will lock you in a concrete cell if you fail to comply with whoever is currently in [[power]]. (Putting it dramatically...)

Like the Romans, conquering everyone in their path, leaving a wasteland behind them, and calling it peace. The anarchist question is whether the we feel now in modern societies is achieved through genuine (democratic) mediation or through temporary bouts of domination by whichever faction effectively controls the state.

An anarchist would argue that versions of "[[liberty]], [[equality]], and fraternity", while being good, fall far short of reality. The gap between stated values and actual practice is a signal of domination rather than genuine cooperation. Still, [[liberty-equality-solidarity|Liberty, equality, and solidarity]] are important to an anarchists but they see them different. The gap between stated values and actual practice is itself evidence that the structure is one of domination rather than genuine cooperation.
