[[Melissa Lott]], former director of research at [[Columbia University]]'s Center on Global Energy Policy, offers a reframe of our current energy paradigm. In 100 or 200 years, she suggests, people will look back and be astonished by how we used energy today.

"Wait, you just burned it?" 

How we were so primitive as a [[combustion]]-based civilization from the vantage point of a future powered by [[solar energy]], [[wind power]], [[nuclear fusion]], [[geothermal energy]], and other clean sources.

The idea is reinforced by [[Bill McKibben]]'s observation that in place of the fires we keep lit day and night, it is possible to rely on the fire already in the sky, three million miles away, whose energy can be collected in photovoltaic panels, and whose differential heating of Earth drives winds that turbines can harness. 

The point is not "a transition away from combustion would be easy", but "from the perspective of what is physically possible, our current energy system is already obsolete". 

We are early in the story of humanity's relationship with energy, not at the end.
