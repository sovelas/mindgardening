[[Martha Nussbaum]] traces the Cynic–Stoic idea of the *kosmou politēs*, the world-citizen. [[Diogenes]] says "I am a citizen of the world", and the [[the Stoics|Stoics]] turn it into a claim about equal worth, that every human has dignity independent of city, class, gender or status.

That's **noble**, and Nussbaum argues that it's the root of our whole idea of equal human dignity.

But the tradition grounds dignity in our *rational and moral capacity*, and then prizes a [[radical-self-sufficiency|radical self-sufficiency]]: virtue alone is good, and the sage's flourishing is untouched by misfortune, poverty, even slavery. So health, food, political freedom, family and all sorts of other external goods a real human needs are pushed aside as morally indifferent.

She follows the thread through [[Cicero]], [[Hugo Grotius]] and [[Adam Smith]], and argues it culminates (once corrected) in her own [[the capabilities approach]].
