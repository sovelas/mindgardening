Amsterdam has one of Europe's worst housing crises despite historically being a world leader in public housing. 

It has a social housing waiting list exceeding a decade, 
average home prices above €600,000, 
the highest median private rents in Europe. 

Nearly 40% of its housing stock is publicly funded affordable housing (comparable to [[Vienna]]) yet the system rewards insiders who secured access early while locking out newcomers and the young.

The crisis is not caused by any single failure but by the interaction of multiple compounding forces: defunded [[woningcorporaties-dutch-housing-associations|housing associations]], [[mortgage-interest-deduction-as-demand-stimulus|mortgage interest deductions]] that inflates prices, [[stacking-requirements-and-construction-cost-crisis|stacking requirements and contruction costs]] that paralyze building, and an [[insider-outsider-dynamic-in-housing|insider/outside dinamic]] that divides the population into protected haves and precarious have-nots. The system combines some of the worst aspects of European [[socialism]] (rigid regulation, long waiting lists) with the worst aspects of [[market economics]] (extractive rents, speculation, wealth inequality).
