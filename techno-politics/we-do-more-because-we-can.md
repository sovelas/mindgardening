Thirty-odd years ago a Game Boy game ran on roughly 1 MB of storage and 10 KB of RAM. The same kind of game today runs on tens of gigabytes — a 100,000× to 1,000,000× jump. The game did not get a hundred thousand times better. We do more because we *can*, not because we *need* — and I'm not sure we stop to reckon (or, with the slow creep of it, even *can* reckon) the harm all that excess and waste does to our lives.

The creep is the dangerous part: no single step looks like the one where you should have stopped, so the threshold gets crossed without anyone deciding to cross it. That's exactly [[counterproductivity|Illich's counterproductivity]] — capacity expanding past the point where it still serves the goal it grew for. And it's the same shape as [[we-just-burned-it|burning fuel simply because it's there]]: abundance with the question of *enough* removed. The cure isn't scarcity; it's [[cuttings-are-free|noticing what you already have]].
