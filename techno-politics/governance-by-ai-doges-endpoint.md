DOGE's logical endpoint was governance by [[artificial intelligence]].
The state reimagined not as a space of [[deliberation]] but as lines of executable code. 

Several concrete steps moved in this direction. 
At the Department of Veterans Affairs, DOGE deployed an AI script to cancel unnecessary contracts. 
DOGE also used AI to locate "diversity, equity, inclusion" language in government policies. 
DOGE announced the "Doge AI Deregulation Decision Tool," promising to cut 100,000 federal regulations within six months. The tool would automate what its creators called the most time-consuming aspect of deregulation: reviewing comments submitted by American citizens. They boasted that hundreds of thousands of public comments could be analysed by AI almost instantly, claiming a 93% reduction in human labour. The implication was that the part of the regulatory process that constituted [[democratic participation]] (citizens submitting their views) was precisely the part targeted for elimination.

Musk reinforced this technocratic framing by wearing a "tech support" T-shirt to cabinet meetings, presenting his role in apolitical terms. But reducing governance to code execution is itself a political act that replaces contestable human judgment with the apparent neutrality of algorithmic output.
