The AI-versus-humanity question keeps snagging me: it feels like it both *should* and *shouldn't* be the case that we give humanity a special kind of tag.

It **should**, because without a special tag dignity has no floor — you slide toward [[heartless-altruism|pure optimisation]], where a human is just a site of utility like any other, tradeable against anything that scores higher. Some line drawn around "persons" seems to be what keeps people from becoming mere means.

It **shouldn't**, because every special tag we've ever drawn has also been a wall — in-group inside, rightless outside. The tag that protects you is the tag that licenses cruelty to whatever falls beyond it: animals, the unborn, one day maybe the machine. And the grounding never sits still: [[dignity-needs-externals|ground the tag in rationality]] and you exclude the humans who most need protecting; ground it in mere needy embodiment and it's not obvious why a future mind that can suffer stays out.

So I don't think the tag is *true*. I think it's a [[symbols-and-the-real|useful fiction we can't yet do without]] — and the danger is forgetting it's a fiction in either direction: worshipping it, or dropping it before we have anything to put in its place.
