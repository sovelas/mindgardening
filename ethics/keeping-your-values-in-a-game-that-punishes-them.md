How do you keep your values in a world that hurts the ones who won't play its nasty games? Somewhere a line has to be drawn — before the hunger for comfort turns you and your ideas into their opposite, before you settle for only what you have and what you can touch. But where?

The hard questions all live on that line. When do your rights start to rise above someone else's? How much can helping another justify hurting yourself — and how much of your own happiness can be traded away to keep someone else from being downgraded? [[freedom-as-imperative|Beauvoir]] says my freedom is bound up with everyone's, which makes the trade-off real but doesn't tell me where to set it.

The rationalisation I most distrust is *if I don't do it, someone else will*. It feels like realism and works like an anaesthetic: it launders the harm by making my particular hand in it look causally irrelevant. But that's true of nearly every harm ever done — if it excused me it would excuse everyone, and then no one is responsible for anything. The line has to be drawn *before* that thought arrives, because that thought will always offer to draw it for you. See also [[empathy-inverts-attention]] and [[ideology-as-religion]].
