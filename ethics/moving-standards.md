Is it just a *standards* thing that alcoholism and poverty get normalised — that what we call a "right" is really just a fight for a bit more than what we currently have? The baseline keeps quietly resetting to wherever we landed, and yesterday's emergency becomes today's acceptable floor.

And does it matter who *decides* the standard? Literally — does it? I suspect I'd strive for it regardless, and I'm not sure much turns on whether I frame it as enlarging *rights* or raising *standards*, or whether I do it for myself or for others.

[[dignity-needs-externals|Nussbaum's worry sits exactly here]]: if dignity needs nothing external, the destitute have no claim on us, and the floor can sink as far as habit allows. Her capabilities move is an attempt to *anchor* the standard to what a human actually needs in order to function — to stop it being a pure ratchet of expectation. [[home-less-ness|Homelessness as a housing problem]] is the same instinct applied: name the concrete deprivation so the standard can't drift. The question I can't close: is there any non-arbitrary floor at all, or is every "right" just a standard we've so far managed to hold? See also [[the-bifurcation-of-duties]].
